# Rules and sources for the finance skill

Read this when the user asks "where does that rule come from?", "what must my invoice show?" or "does this apply to me?". It lists the official pages this skill points to, what each one covers, and what the skill does not know.

**This is general information, not tax, legal, accounting or investment advice.** The pages below are named so the user can read the rules for themselves. This file does not give tax rates, registration thresholds, filing deadlines or record-keeping periods on purpose: they differ by country, they change, and a wrong figure costs money. Open the current page, and ask a qualified accountant or tax adviser about a specific case.

All pages were opened on 2026-10-27. They are described here in general terms, not quoted.

## United Kingdom: HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC), on GOV.UK

1. **Invoicing and taking payment from customers**
   https://www.gov.uk/invoicing-and-taking-payment-from-customers
   What an invoice must include, the extra details for sole traders and limited companies, and how invoices and payments work in general.

2. **VAT record keeping: VAT invoices**
   https://www.gov.uk/vat-record-keeping/vat-invoices
   What a VAT invoice must show for a VAT-registered business, the different kinds of VAT invoice, and what a credit note must include when an invoice is corrected.

3. **Register for VAT**
   https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration
   When a business must or may register for VAT. The skill never states the threshold: the user reads it here.

4. **Business records if you're self-employed**
   https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-records
   Which records a self-employed person must keep, and for how long.

5. **Expenses if you're self-employed**
   https://www.gov.uk/expenses-if-youre-self-employed
   HMRC's groups of allowable expenses, and the rule for costs that are part business and part personal.

6. **Cash basis**
   https://www.gov.uk/simpler-income-tax-cash-basis
   How the cash basis works for self-employed people, compared with recording income when invoiced.

## United States: Internal Revenue Service (IRS)

7. **Publication 583, Starting a Business and Keeping Records**
   https://www.irs.gov/publications/p583
   Record-keeping for a small business, accounting periods and methods, and which supporting documents to keep.

8. **Recordkeeping (small businesses and self-employed)**
   https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/recordkeeping
   Why records matter and what kinds of records to keep.

9. **How long should I keep records?**
   https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-long-should-i-keep-records
   How long records should be kept, which depends on the situation. The skill never states the period: the user reads it here.

10. **About Schedule C (Form 1040), Profit or Loss from Business**
    https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-schedule-c-form-1040
    The form and instructions sole proprietors use to report business income and expenses, including its own expense categories.

11. **State government websites**
    https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/state-government-websites
    In the US, sales tax is set by states and local governments, not by the IRS. This IRS page links to each state's site.

## European Union: the VAT Directive and the European Commission

12. **Council Directive 2006/112/EC on the common system of value added tax** (EUR-Lex)
    https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2006/112/oj
    The EU VAT Directive. Its articles on invoicing (Title XI, Chapter 3) set the details a VAT invoice must show across the EU; each member state applies them through its own national law.

13. **Your Europe: VAT invoicing rules** (European Commission)
    https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/taxation/vat/invoicing-rules/index_en.htm
    A plain summary of the EU invoicing rules for businesses, with links to national tax authorities.

## Every other country

Use the national tax authority's own website, found from the government's main site, not from a search advert or a third-party summary. If you cannot open it, say so and write `[TO CONFIRM: rules for <country>, from the tax authority or an accountant]`.

## What this skill does not know

- **Rates, thresholds and deadlines.** It never states a tax rate, a registration threshold, a filing deadline or a record-keeping period. The user gives them, or they stay `[TO CONFIRM]`.
- **Whether a cost is allowable.** The categories in `expense-categories.md` are for tidy books. What a tax authority allows is the accountant's call.
- **The user's situation.** Residence, business structure, other income, regional rules and special schemes all change the answer. The skill does not ask about all of them and does not try to.
- **Changes after the date above.** Pages and rules change. Open the current page before relying on it.
