# Expense categories: the starter list

Read this when you categorise expenses (Step 2 of `SKILL.md`). It gives a starter list of categories, says how to mark a line you are unsure of, and lists the lines you flag instead of deciding.

These categories are for keeping the books tidy. They are not a statement of what a tax authority allows as a business expense: that depends on the country and on the business, and it is the accountant's call. If the user's accountant gave them a list of categories, or their accounting app has its own, use that list instead, word for word.

## The starter list

| Category | What usually goes in it | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Cost of goods | Stock, materials and parts bought to sell or to make what is sold | Items kept for the user's own use |
| Subcontractors and freelancers | People or firms paid to do part of the work | Payments to employees, which are usually payroll, not this |
| Software and subscriptions | Apps, hosting, domains, online tools | Personal subscriptions on the same card |
| Equipment | Computers, tools, machines, furniture for the business | Larger items the accountant may treat differently from day-to-day costs: flag them |
| Office and supplies | Stationery, printing, small items used up in the work | |
| Rent and premises | Office, studio, shop or storage rent, and their bills | Working from home: a share of home costs is the accountant's call, so flag it |
| Phone and internet | Business lines and plans | Lines used both for personal and business calls: flag them |
| Travel | Train, plane, taxi, fuel, parking, hotels for business trips | Commuting and mixed trips: flag them |
| Meals and entertainment | Meals while travelling for work, client meals | Rules on these vary a lot between countries: always mark "unsure" and flag |
| Marketing and advertising | Ads, printing, sponsorships, website costs | |
| Professional fees | Accountant, lawyer, other advisers | |
| Bank and payment fees | Account fees, card and payment processing fees | |
| Insurance | Business insurance policies | Personal policies paid from the same account |
| Training | Courses and books for the business | Training for a new, unrelated line of work: flag it |
| Other | Anything that fits no line above | Always "unsure", with a reason |

## Lines you flag instead of deciding

These are not expenses, or not only expenses, or their treatment depends on rules you must not guess. Give them the category "Flagged" (or the nearest category with `flag_for_accountant: yes`) and a one-line reason.

- Transfers between the user's own accounts
- Owner's drawings, or money the owner pays in
- Loan repayments and loan money received
- Tax payments of any kind, and refunds from a tax authority
- Refunds to or from customers and suppliers
- Anything that might be personal, or part personal and part business
- Amounts in another currency, or with a conversion fee
- Lines whose description you cannot read or understand

## Sure or unsure

- **Sure** only when the description and the amount leave no doubt about the category, for example a monthly charge from a known hosting company.
- **Unsure** for everything else, with a reason the user can answer in a word: "Is this a client meal or personal?", "What is 'TRF 2291'?".
- If more than a few lines are unsure, say so at the top of your answer, so the user knows to check before using the totals.

## How to read a bank or app export

- Check the date format with the user if it could be read two ways (for example 03/04).
- Check whether money out is shown as negative numbers, in a separate column, or with a marker. Say how you read it.
- Never drop a line. If a line looks like a duplicate, keep it and flag it.
- Never round an amount. Keep the number of decimals given.
- Keep the user's description text as it is, so they can find the line in their bank or app.
