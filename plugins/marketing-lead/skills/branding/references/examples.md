# Filled examples: voice card, naming shortlist, brand guideline

Read this when the user wants to see what a finished deliverable looks like (Steps 3 to 5 of `SKILL.md`). The business below is made up: a freelance translator who works alone, here called "Example Translations". Nothing in this file is a real business, customer or search result.

## 1. Voice card (filled)

```
VOICE CARD: Example Translations                  Last reviewed: [date]
WHO WE TALK TO  Small firms that need contracts and web pages in a second language,
                usually close to a deadline ("we launch next week and the site is only in English")

VOICE (never changes)
| Word  | This (in writing)                                 | Not that                               |
| Clear | Short sentences, numbers in figures, one ask      | Blunt or cold                          |
| Calm  | States the problem and the next step              | Alarmed, or vague to soften bad news   |
| Warm  | Uses the client's name, thanks them for a reason  | Gushing, emoji walls, fake familiarity |

TONE (1 to 5)   funny-serious | formal-casual | respectful-irreverent | enthusiastic-matter-of-fact
Default         4 | 3 | 1 | 3
Bad news        5 | 2 | 1 | 5
Launch          3 | 4 | 2 | 2
Payment         5 | 3 | 1 | 4

WORDS WE USE    "deadline", "your launch", "the final file", "ready to publish"
WORDS WE AVOID  "leverage", "solutions", "world-class", "seamless", "synergy"
RULES           active voice; "you"; one ask per message; numbers in figures; dates as "Friday 14 March"

BEFORE  It has come to our attention that payment for invoice #214 has not yet been
        received. Please be advised that late fees may be applied in accordance with our terms.
AFTER   Hi Sam, invoice #214 is still open. Could you pay it by Friday? If something is holding
        it up, just reply and we'll sort it out. A late fee applies after that, as in our terms.
WHAT CHANGED  Active voice ("invoice #214 is still open"). The reader's name and "you".
              One ask with one date and one way out. The fee is still there: calm, not vague.

VOICE BRIEF
Write for small firms with a translation deadline. Our voice is clear, not blunt; calm, not
vague; warm, not gushing. Default tone: serious 4, casual 3, respectful 1, matter-of-fact 3.
For bad news: more serious and matter-of-fact, no jokes. Use: deadline, your launch, final
file. Never use: leverage, solutions, world-class, seamless. Rules: active voice, "you", one
ask per message, numbers in figures. Example we like: the AFTER text above. Before you hand
back a draft, remove anything on the "never" list.
```

## 3. One-page brand guideline (filled)

The ratios were computed with the WCAG 2.2 formula in `SKILL.md` and are shown to two decimals; confirm them in any WCAG contrast checker before relying on them.

```
BRAND GUIDELINES: Example Translations          Version 1, decided on [date] by [the user]

1. LOGO     Files: full | icon | white version for dark backgrounds   Made by: [who]; rights: [TO CONFIRM]
            Clear space: the height of the "E" on every side         Minimum width: 96 px on screen
            Never: stretch | recolour | add shadows | place on a busy photo
2. COLOURS  #1F2933 on #FFFFFF   14.76:1   body text
            #0F766E on #FFFFFF    5.47:1   links (underlined) and headings
            #1F2933 on #14B8A6    5.93:1   buttons
            Accent #14B8A6: never with white text on it (white on #14B8A6 is 2.49:1, fails)
            Rule: colour is never the only signal (links underlined, errors in words)
3. TYPE     Headings: one sans-serif, bold   Body: same family, regular, at least 16 px
            Licence read for: web y | print y | social y | app n/a | logo y   on [date]
4. VOICE    Clear, not blunt. Calm, not vague. Warm, not gushing. Full card: [link]
5. IMAGES   Style: ink illustrations with one teal accent   Never: stock photos of handshakes
            People: real people with written permission only; AI-made images labelled
            Alt text on every informative image; alt="" on decoration; no text in images except the logo
TO CONFIRM  Logo rights; font licence for print
```

What the colour rows teach: the bright teal `#14B8A6` fails with white text and passes with dark ink. The user keeps the colour; the guideline records what may sit on top of it.
