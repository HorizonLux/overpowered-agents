# Rules and sources for the reputation skill

Read this when the user asks "where does that rule come from?" or "does this apply to me?". It lists every official page the skill relies on, the exact words used, and what the skill does not know.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules and platform policies change: open the page and read the current version before relying on it, and ask a lawyer about a specific case.

All pages were opened on 2026-10-23. "Undated" means the page shows no date other than a copyright year. Quotes are copied from the page; curly apostrophes may appear as straight ones.

## United States: Federal Trade Commission (FTC)

**1. FTC press release, "Federal Trade Commission Announces Final Rule Banning Fake Reviews and Testimonials"** (2024-08-14)
https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials
- Fake reviews: reviews that "misrepresent that they are by someone who does not exist, such as AI-generated fake reviews" (part of a sentence).
- Incentives: "providing compensation or other incentives conditioned on the writing of consumer reviews expressing a particular sentiment" (part of a sentence).
- Insider reviews: "reviews and testimonials written by company insiders that fail to clearly and conspicuously disclose" (part of a sentence; it continues with the giver's material connection to the business).
- Company-controlled review sites: "misrepresenting that a website or entity it controls provides independent reviews or opinions" (part of a sentence).
- Review suppression: "using unfounded or groundless legal threats, physical threats, intimidation, or certain false public accusations" (part of a sentence about preventing or removing negative reviews).

**2. FTC, "The Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule: Questions and Answers"** (November 2024)
https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers
- Effective date: "which went into effect on October 21, 2024" (part of a sentence).
- Penalties: "The rule authorizes courts to impose civil penalties for knowing violations" (part of a sentence).
- Incentives: "The rule does not prohibit giving incentives for reviews" (part of a sentence; the condition is that no particular sentiment is required). The next sentence: "But remember that failing to disclose incentives could be a violation of the FTC Act." Platform rules can be stricter: Google, Yelp and Trustpilot ban incentives for reviews (see below).
- False accusations: "The rule prohibits making a false accusation about the reviewer if you know that the accusation is false" (part of a sentence), which continues "or make it with reckless disregard as to its truth or falsity."
- Threats: "Your response also shouldn't use physical threats or intimidation to get the review (or a portion of it) removed or changed."
- Paying for removal: "paying consumers to change or remove truthful negative reviews may violate the FTC Act" (part of a sentence).
- Asking only happy customers: "The rule does not contain a specific prohibition against such conduct." and "But this practice could violate the FTC Act."

**3. FTC, "Soliciting and Paying for Online Reviews: A Guide for Marketers"** (January 2022)
https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/soliciting-paying-online-reviews-guide-marketers
- "Don't ask for reviews only from customers you think will leave positive ones."
- "If you offer an incentive for a review, don't condition it, explicitly or implicitly, on the review being positive." The guide adds that the review should disclose the incentive.
- "Don't ask your staff to write reviews of your business," unless they disclose the connection.
- "Don't ask family and friends for reviews," unless they disclose the connection.
- "Don't ask for reviews from people who haven't used or experienced the product or service."

## Google (Google Business Profile and Google Maps reviews)

**4. Google Maps User Generated Content Policy, "Prohibited & restricted content"** (undated)
https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114
- Merchants may not "Offer incentives – such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services - in exchange for posting any review" (start of a longer bullet).
- Merchants may not "Discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers".
- Merchants may not "Solicit or encourage the posting of content that does not represent a genuine experience."
- "Content that is based on a conflict of interest." is listed under rating manipulation and misrepresentation.
- "Do not distribute or post personal information without consent."

**5. Google Business Profile Help, "Manage customer reviews"** (undated)
https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050
- "If your reply is approved, it will be publicly posted under the customer's review."
- Replies are reviewed: "This makes sure that they follow Google's content policies."
- "Helpful and positive replies to reviews can show that you're responsive to your customers."
- "To encourage customers to leave reviews, you can share a review request link or QR code."
- "If you believe that a review violates Google's content policies, you can flag it."

**6. Google Business Profile Help, "Tips to get more reviews"** (undated)
https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122
- "Be professional and polite: Keep your replies clear and helpful."
- "Keep it short and simple: Customers value direct and honest responses."
- "A genuine apology can go a long way in rebuilding trust."
- "Respond in a timely manner: A prompt response shows that you value your customers' feedback and are committed to high-quality service."
- "avoid using your response to offer deals or promotions." (part of a sentence)
- On incentives for reviews: "is considered fake & misleading content and is strictly prohibited." (part of a sentence)

**7. Google Search Help, "Create an alert"** (undated)
https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/4815696
- "You can get emails when new results for a topic show up in Google Search."
- Steps: "Go to Google Alerts." (https://www.google.com/alerts) / "In the box at the top, enter a topic you want to follow." / "To change your settings, click Show options." / "Click Create Alert."
- Settings listed: how often you get notifications, the types of sites, language, the part of the world, how many results, which accounts get the alert.

## Yelp

**8. Yelp for Business, "Don't Ask for Reviews"** (undated)
https://biz.yelp.com/support-center/Reviews/Best_Practices/Don-t-Ask-for-Reviews/en-US
- "Proactively asking for reviews may hurt your Yelp rating"
- "Don't ask anyone to review your business, be it customers, mailing list subscribers, friends, family, etc."
- "Yelp's software tries to identify any reviews that appear to have been requested, and not recommend them."
- "Don't offer freebies, discounts, or payment in exchange for reviews"
- "For the same reason, you can't offer incentives for users to remove reviews."

## Trustpilot

**9. Trustpilot, "Guidelines for businesses"** (version 7.3, September 2026)
https://corporate.trustpilot.com/legal/for-businesses/guidelines-for-businesses
- Not allowed: "Be selective with invitations, by picking and choosing which customers you invite."
- Not allowed: "Inviting only those people who you know have had a positive experience"
- Expected: "Invite consistently and fairly — this means inviting everyone in the same way," and "regardless of whether they've had a positive or negative experience with your business" (parts of sentences).
- Not allowed: "Offer incentives in connection with writing or editing reviews."
- "We don't allow incentivized reviews on Trustpilot."

## United Kingdom

**10. Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, Schedule 20, paragraph 13** (legislation.gov.uk)
https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/schedule/20
- Schedule heading: "Commercial practices which are in all circumstances considered unfair"
- 13(1): "Submitting, or commissioning another person to submit or write—" (a) "a fake consumer review", (b) "a consumer review that conceals the fact it has been incentivised."
- 13(2): "Publishing consumer reviews, or consumer review information, in a misleading way."
- 13(5)(c): "a consumer review conceals the fact it has been incentivised if—" (i) "a person has been commissioned to submit or write the review, and" (ii) "that fact is not made apparent (whether through the contents of the review or otherwise);"
- 13(5)(g): "“commissioning” includes incentivising by any means (and “commissioned” is to be read accordingly);"
- What this means for the skill: a business that offers a reward for a review is "commissioning" it, so the business must make sure every such review makes the reward apparent. Leaving it to the reviewer is not enough. The page was the latest revised version, with no outstanding change listed for paragraph 13.

**11. Competition and Markets Authority, "Unfair commercial practices" (CMA207)** (updated 18 November 2025)
https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unfair-commercial-practices-cma207/unfair-commercial-practices
- Lists banned practice 13, including submitting or commissioning "a fake consumer review" and "a consumer review that conceals the fact it has been incentivised", and "Offering services to traders to either: submit or commission fake reviews".

## European Union

**12. Your Europe (official EU portal), "Unfair and blacklisted commercial practices"** (last checked by the EU 28/04/2026)
https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/consumers/unfair-treatment/unfair-commercial-practices/index_en.htm
- "traders publishing reviews must disclose how they ensure the reviews have been submitted by real consumers"
- "These rules explicitly prohibit traders from altering customer reviews and ratings of their products or services."
- "traders are also prohibited from paying people to write fake reviews or make endorsements"

## What this skill does not know (UNVERIFIED)

- **The EU legal text.** The consumer-review points added to Annex I of Directive 2005/29/EC (by Directive (EU) 2019/2161) could not be read: EUR-Lex returned an empty page on 2026-10-23. The EU line in the skill rests on the Your Europe portal only.
- **EU rules on incentivised reviews.** UNVERIFIED: no source in this file covers rewards for reviews in the EU (the Your Europe page above has no sentence on it; searched 2026-10-23; reason: no primary source read). The skill does not say whether an incentivised review is allowed in the EU.
- **The US regulation itself** (16 CFR Part 465) was not opened; the FTC facts rest on the FTC's own press release, Q&A and guide.
- **Other countries.** Rules outside the US, UK and EU are not covered. Point the user to their national consumer-protection authority.
- **Sector confidentiality rules** (for example health privacy law) and how they apply to review replies were not checked. The skill's "confidential work" template is a cautious default, not a statement of any law.
- **Other platforms** (Facebook, app stores, marketplaces, industry sites) were not checked. Before asking for reviews on them, read that platform's current review policy.
- **Numbers.** The skill has no verified figure for how many people read reviews, how much replies help, or how many mentions free alerts miss, so it gives none.
